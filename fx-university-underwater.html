FX UNIVERSITY / RESEARCH NOTE 01

Drawdown Is Depth.
Underwater Is Time.

A trading strategy can suffer a drawdown that looks manageable and still fail a very different test: how long you have to wait before seeing a new high.

~14.9 years
The underwater period observed in the QuickShift long-run research simulation.

One of the most important moments in FX University arrived when a strategy that looked increasingly promising was tested over a much longer window.

The maximum drawdown in that research simulation was about 11%. On its own, that number did not look catastrophic. The problem was not the depth of the decline.

The problem was the time.

Two questions that sound similar, but are not

Maximum drawdown
How far did the strategy fall from a previous high?

Underwater period
How long did the strategy remain below that previous high before recovering?

A strategy can answer the first question reasonably well and still fail the second one badly. In the QuickShift long-run test, the MTM equity curve reached a high on September 22, 2011 and had not made a new high by the end of July 2026.

That is 5,426 days—roughly 14.9 years.

The strategy had not sunk especially deep. It had simply decided to live underwater.

Why time changes the decision

Drawdown is usually discussed as a risk number. But time is a human constraint.

If a system takes fifteen years to recover, the question is no longer only whether the account survives. The question becomes whether the person operating it can still believe in it, keep allocating capital to it, resist changing it, and wait.

That is especially relevant when the original reason for building the system was to create more freedom now—not decades later.

What changed in FX University

This result helped change the research question from:

“Can we build one excellent automated FX strategy?”

to something closer to:

“Can we build a portfolio of strategies with different sources of return and different ways of failing?”

That is where the “professors” stop being just a joke. A university with multiple professors is harder to kill than a university built around one star.

The larger lesson

Backtesting is not only a search for attractive metrics. It is a search for ways your idea can disappoint you before real money has to do the teaching.

AI can make it much cheaper for a non-programmer to implement hypotheses and run experiments. That does not make skepticism less important. It makes skepticism more important, because producing another plausible strategy becomes easier.
